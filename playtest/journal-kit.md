# Kit's playtest journal

## Before the game

Okay. I have finished Expedition 33, all of it, every act, and I have cried at the appropriate moments and a few inappropriate ones. I have never played Root. I have barely played any board game that isn't Uno. So this is me reading a menu of my favourite people dressed up as something I don't know how to play.

### First reaction

My honest first reaction is delight, followed quickly by "what is a clearing". The names are all right. The tone is right. Someone who loves this game wrote the blurbs. But about half of every card is a language I don't speak yet.

### What I understood

The shape of every win, which is nice because it is the same shape six times. You meet a list of conditions at the end of your own turn, the table gets warned, and then if it is all still true when your next turn starts, you win. So the whole game is "get your thing set up, then survive everyone else's turn without them wrecking it." I get that. That is a heist. I like heists.

I also understood that there is a fallback of 30 VP that works for everybody. I am guessing VP is victory points, because everything else on the card says "VP" like I am supposed to know.

I understood that the three big factions (Gustave, Paintress, Sciel) each want to hold clearings (places on the map) and put things in them, and that the Vagabond people (Esquie, Verso, Maelle) are different. They talk about a single "pawn" and about "items" and being "Allied" or "Hostile" with other players, so I think a Vagabond is one person walking around the board rather than an army. That sounds easier to keep track of, but that is a guess.

The Expedition 33 bits I could follow without any board game knowledge:
- Gustave plants Expedition Flags, like the flags you rest at. Lumière starts flagged. Lovely.
- Gustave's Arm and "Overcharge". Yes! That is his actual thing. His charge going up and then getting spent is his whole fighting rhythm.
- Paintress painting a lower number on the Monolith every year. Correct. Chilling, and nobody has to explain it to me.
- Verso's Perfection rank going D, C, B, A, S. That is Verso's real thing too, and I love that it got turned into a clean-fight meter instead of just a number.
- Sciel's win being called "Twilight Harvest". Twilight is very much a Sciel word. I like it. Farm and lanterns and the sun going down is her.
- Maelle's win is called "A Home in the Canvas". That title gave me a feeling, and I won't say why, because the card is on safe mode and I don't want to ruin it for someone else. If you know, you know.
- "The Family Portrait" for the Paintress. Same. Safe mode, but I grinned.

### What I did not understand (words that lost me)

I'm going to quote them, because I think a new player would stop at every one of these.

- **"Each character plays a base-game Root faction."** What is a Root faction? I don't know what base-game means here or what "Marquise de Cat" is beyond a cat with a title. The line "rides the Marquise de Cat" confused me for a second. Rides?
- **"ruled" / "rules a clearing" / "ruled, flagged clearings"** shows up everywhere and is never explained. Does ruling mean most pieces? Does it mean I own it? On the Paintress's card it says "ties count, as Lords of the Forest." Lords of the Forest means nothing to me. I don't know if that is a rule, a card, a joke, or a thing I'm supposed to know.
- **"roost"** (Paintress). A roost is where a bird sleeps. Is it a building? She has "Living Portraits" made of roosts. Fun idea, but I couldn't draw it.
- **"Birdsong"** ("wins at the start of his next Birdsong"). Nobody tells me what this is. A time of day? A phase? I assumed it is the start of your turn, but that is only a guess from context.
- **"Canvas Plan"** (Paintress). "Bound by her own Canvas Plan." Cool phrase. I have no idea what it makes her do.
- **"Chain" and "Beachhead"** (Gustave). Beachhead I can picture, because the Monolith is on the coast. Chain is a list of flagged clearings connected by paths, I think, but I had to read it three times.
- **"Sanctuary"** (Sciel). Defined as "a clearing with her Lantern and no enemy pieces of any kind," which is fine, but then the win text says "no enemy building or token," and the warning text says "pieces of any kind." Is that the same? Is a token different from a piece? I also don't know what an Aquafarm is or who puts it on the map. "At least one Aquafarm is on the map" sounds like it might not even be hers to place. That worries me.
- **"coalition"** ("No win while he is in a coalition"), on all three Vagabonds. Never explained.
- **"Allied" / "Hostile"** (Maelle). Allied with whom, and how? Do I get there by talking, or by giving things away?
- **"items damaged" / "exhausted items"**. Damaged I get. Exhausted items count as undamaged? I don't know what exhausted means for an item. Verso's card says "-1 if 1-2 [items] were damaged." Does that mean I have to choose which battles to take based on my gear? I think so, but I'm not sure.
- **"warriors," "buildings," "pawns," "tokens"**. Four different kinds of thing and I only know three of them.
- **"dominance card"** ("Every character can also win the Root way: 30 VP, or a dominance card"). No idea.
- **"On Easy, a human Verso at rank A with 2 undamaged swords counts as S."** What is Easy? Is there a difficulty setting for the table? It only appears on Verso's card and I'm not sure why a person should care.
- **"From Act II with 10+ VP."** I know Act II as the second act of the story. I don't know if that is a point in the game, such as turn number or a phase, or something that happens when a thing occurs. If a new player reads "from Act II" they will think story.
- **Esquie's rocks list.** Florrie waits at Stone Wave Cliffs. Dorrie is in Old Lumière from Act II. Soarrie "appears in Act II on Verso's card, or with whoever rules the Monolith." Urrie is at Esquie's Nest from Act III. That is a lot of clauses for what is, underneath, "go and collect four things." I couldn't tell what "bring home" means. Home where? Also, "the White-Haired Man's warriors stand in Old Lumière" is the only mention of a player who isn't at the table, and I know exactly who he is, but I can't see how he is going to end up with warriors there if nobody is seated as him. Is he a computer player? Is he always there?

### Do they sound like themselves?

Mostly yes, and that's the thing I'm most pleased about.

**Gustave:** An engineer with a mechanical arm, a leader who builds things, and a road to the Monolith. He is a planner who fixes things and tells people to go on. His "Overcharge" is a good fit. One small snag, and it is mine, not the game's: I know where Gustave's story goes. The card says he wins "from Act II." In my head Gustave does not make it to Act II. I'm not saying the game is wrong, because it is a mod and a board game, but it made me smile and wince at the same time. I want to see how the game handles it.

**The Paintress:** The best-written card, voice-wise. "Every year she paints a lower number on the Monolith, and every expedition, Expedition 33 included, sets out to stop her." That is the correct opening. Easel, Monolith, portraits: yes.

**Sciel:** Warm. Farmer, children, a future. Fits. But the card doesn't say anything about her tarot cards, which are the first thing I think of with Sciel. She's Foretell and cards and the setting sun. Lanterns and a harvest feel gentle and farm-like, so maybe that is the Sciel the game wants. I would not have guessed Woodland Alliance for her from the name alone.

**Esquie:** Cheerful, gentle, "never gets angry." That is him. Carrying the expedition anywhere is what he does in the game. I like that his win is "bring my friends' rocks home" and no one has to be defeated. I'm less sure about "Tinker." I would not have called Esquie a tinkerer. He's a very big, very happy flying creature who loves food.

**Verso:** "A calm stranger with a sword and a pistol who seems to know the Continent far too well." Good, and I like that it winks without spilling anything. "Stand alone in the Monolith" is spooky and right. "Ranger" fits him better than I expected.

**Maelle:** "Sixteen-year-old foster sister," youngest on the expedition, wants somewhere to belong. That's right. "Win two factions over as allies, make no enemies" does not sound entirely like the Maelle I remember, who can be sharp and stubborn and fights hard when she's hurt. But "fencer" is right, and "hold her ground in Lumière" is a good place to put her. "Thief" for Maelle is the one I can't explain. Maybe the idea is that she steals her way into people's hearts. I'll see.

### What excites me

- That Verso and the Paintress want the **same place**, the Monolith, but opposite things there. She wants to hold it, and he wants to stand alone in it. Whoever sits next to whom at the table, that is going to be tense, and given who they are to each other, it is correct.
- Gustave's flags and the Road. I love that he has a physical route that people can see and cut.
- Esquie's win is gentle and does not need any points. If everyone else is fighting about the Monolith and he just quietly walks around collecting his friends' rocks, that is the best thing that could happen at the table.

### What worries me

- I don't know what I'm doing, and **the cards are the only teacher I've got**. Words like "ruled," "roost," "token," and "Birdsong" are used as if I've already learned them.
- Gustave, Paintress, and Sciel all make me hold things, with other players actively trying to stop me. Three of the Vagabond-ish cards say "No win while he is in a coalition," which sounds like if the table gangs up on you, you can't win. I don't know how I would stop that.
- I only get one pick, and the table is four with only one Vagabond, so if Jo (first pick) takes a Vagabond, the other two I love are gone for the entire game. If Jo takes one of the three factions, I probably still get to choose between the Vagabonds.
- Verso and Maelle both ask me to keep my items undamaged. I don't know how fast things get damaged, so I don't know how fragile that is.

### My ranking, and why

I pick second, so I should assume Jo grabs my favourite. I've put these in the order I'd want them, and I mean it.

1. **verso.** My favourite character to play, and the cleanest "feeling" on the cards. One walker, one clear goal (rank S and stand alone in the Monolith), a mood of "I know where this is going." He's also in direct conflict with the Paintress, which is exactly the thing I want to see play out.
2. **maelle.** A Home in the Canvas. It is the one I can feel the most, and "make friends, avoid enemies" is something I think I can learn even without knowing the rules. She is a little less like herself than Verso is, but she is close.
3. **gustave.** My brain says he's the lead and the nicest to play: flags, a road, a clear arm meter. But his card is wall-to-wall jargon (Chain, Beachhead, ruled), so I'd be learning the most words at once.
4. **sciel.** I love her, and the Twilight Harvest title is good. But "Sanctuary" has two different definitions on the same card, and the Aquafarm is a thing I can't control, which sounds like a way to lose a game I understand.
5. **esquie.** He is such a nice idea, and it is the easiest win in plain English ("four rocks home"). But the actual clauses are a maze (Act II here, Act III there, a White-Haired Man blocking one of them), and "Tinker" doesn't sound like him. I'd happily play him just for the company if I'm last pick.
6. **paintress.** I want to be the Paintress. I really do; the card is the best one. She's last only because it is the most jargon I would be walking into with the least idea of how to play it ("roost," "Lords of the Forest," "Canvas Plan," "ties count"). If I knew the rules, she'd be near the top. I'd rather someone who knows what a roost is has her, so that I have somebody to learn from.

(I would not be heartbroken if I end up with anyone. I'm mostly curious whether the first few turns teach me the words, or whether I'm going to be asking the table every five minutes.)

## Round 1 (my first turn, as Gustave)

Setup first. The game asked me three small questions: where to put a sawmill, a workshop and a recruiter. I did not know what any of the three do. The log taught me a little ("Birdsong: place one wood at each sawmill" sounds like sawmills make wood, and wood pays for building). I put the sawmill at home in Lumière (safe, it's the keep), the workshop in Forgotten Battlefield because it is a mouse clearing and the Road card says I need fox, rabbit and mouse, and the recruiter in Spring Meadows because it is a little dead end with two roads in. That was me guessing.

I liked that my first line of speech came out unprompted and in character: "Thirty-two, one year left, and now the whole world takes turns. Fine. I'll make mine count." That is Gustave. The second one in Birdsong ("Whoever painted this world gave it rules, a turn order and a scoring track. Very tidy. I'd still like a word with them.") is also very him, dry and practical. Sciel's "keep the young ones out of harm's way" is Sciel. Esquie's "I'm much too round for squares" made me laugh out loud. The Paintress being polite about painting the world carefully is chilling in the right way.

What I saw others do: Sciel put sympathy tokens in three clearings and mobilised (I think that means putting cards face-down in a stack). Esquie slipped to Stone Wave Cliffs and crafted a tea, which scored 2 VP right away. The Paintress started with 6 warriors and a roost in the Monolith (clearing 3) and 2 warriors in Stone Wave Cliffs, which is where Florrie is. So the Paintress is already "guarding" Esquie's first rock. Spicy.

My turn: the game dropped me straight into Daylight with 3 actions and a 36-line menu. That was a lot. What I worked out:
- I rule almost the whole map because I have 1 warrior everywhere. Ruling matters for building and (I think) for winning.
- I battled Sciel's sympathy token in Old Lumière. I had no idea what would happen. I rolled 2 and 2, she had no warriors, so I got +1 for "defenseless", and the token got removed. +1 VP. Gustave "fights a thing that is not fighting back" felt a bit off for Gustave, but he is a pragmatist.
- THEN: "Outrage ... give a fox card (birds count) to Sciel's Supporters stack." Nobody warned me. I had to hand over a card. I gave Ambush! and kept Royal Claim. Lesson learned the hard way: hitting Sciel's tokens costs me a card each time. I did not do more battles because I only had Mouse Dominance and Royal Claim left and I did not want to lose either.
- I built a workshop in Old Lumière (cost 1 wood, 2 VP, now 3 VP) and recruited one warrior.

What I think I now know about my hand: Royal Claim says "In Birdsong, you may discard this card to score one point per clearing you rule." I rule ten clearings. That is 10 points if nobody takes anything off me before my next Birdsong. I am keeping it for that. Also Mouse Dominance: I rule two mouse clearings already (7 and 12), and I need a third plus 10 VP. That looks like a real route to winning.

Possible bug / confusion to flag: I had Royal Claim in hand during my Birdsong this turn and was never offered to discard it, I just got the wood automatically and then Daylight. I will watch whether it prompts next turn. (If it is supposed to be offered automatically, it did not show up. Command: just "wait", no command of mine.)

Also: the craft step. The rules say craft first in Daylight, but there was no craft option because nothing in my hand was craftable (Royal Claim costs any+any+any+any, which is four, I have two workshops). The game did not say "you have nothing to craft", it just skipped. Fine, but a new player may wonder.

Other confusion: tokens do not take up slots (Esquie's Nest says "1 slot, 1 free" with a sympathy token in it). I guessed it, the game does not say so.

Feels like Root with Expedition 33 flavour: the speech bubbles and the names. The Arm charge, flags and Road are all there in the goal tracker but nothing has happened with them yet since it is Act I. I do not feel like an engineer yet, I feel like I am doing bookkeeping.
