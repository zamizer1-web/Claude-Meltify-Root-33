# Jo's playtest journal

## Before the game

So this is the character screen. Six people, and I get to pick first, which feels like a lot of pressure when I don't know what any of it means. The top says "Each character plays a base-game Root faction" and I have never played Root, so that sentence is already over my head. I'm going to read each card like it's the back of a box and pick the one where I can at least tell what I'm supposed to DO.

**What I got from the cards (the bits that make sense):**
- Every card has a "How they win" part, and they all seem to be a "get a bunch of specific things and then hold them until your next turn" deal. The game calls it a "countdown win." I think that means you do the thing, everyone gets a warning, and if nobody stops you by your next turn you win. That I like. It's like a heads-up in a card game: "I'm about to go out, stop me."
- Most of them say "From Act II with 10+ VP." I don't know what an Act is, and I have no idea what VP is. Points, I'm guessing, because the bottom says "30 VP still wins," so it's probably victory points. Nobody told me how you get them though.
- The last line says "Every character can also win the Root way: 30 VP, or a dominance card." I don't know what a dominance card is. Sounds like a card you play to win. Not explained.

**Words that lost me (quoting them straight off the screen):**
- "Marquise de Cat", "Eyrie Dynasties", "Woodland Alliance", "Vagabond" (and "Tinker", "Ranger", "Thief"). Are these the teams? Roles? No clue.
- "ruled clearing" / "rules a clearing" / "ties count, as Lords of the Forest". What does it mean to RULE a place? How do you rule it?
- "roost", "Living Portrait", "warriors", "Birdsong" ("at the start of his next Birdsong"). I'm guessing Birdsong is a part of a turn, but it's not explained.
- "Beachhead (a clearing next to the Monolith)". At least that one got a bracket. The Monolith I get a bit: it's the big scary painted thing in the middle that the Paintress paints numbers on.
- "Expedition Flag", "Chain", "Gustave's Arm (0-3)", "Overcharge". The Arm gaining charges only "when he starts a battle that removes an enemy piece"... I can't picture it.
- "Lantern", "Aquafarm", "Sanctuary". The Sanctuary one was explained ("a clearing with her Lantern and no enemy pieces of any kind") which helped, but I still don't know what a Lantern is or how I light one.
- "coalition" ("No win while he is in a coalition"). Not explained. I'm guessing it's a team-up against someone, but that's a guess.
- "Allied", "Hostile", "undamaged items (exhausted items count)". Items are things I carry, I think? What does exhausted mean? Tired? Used?
- "pawn", "guards", "the White-Haired Man's warriors", "Verso's card", "Act III". Never heard of any of it.
- "Easy" in Verso's card: "On Easy, a human Verso at rank A..." Is there an easy mode? Is it on right now? No idea.

**Ranking, and why:**

1. **Esquie**: This is the only card that sounds like a game I already know how to play. "Find four lost rocks and bring them home" is a scavenger hunt. Four named rocks (Florrie, Dorrie, Soarrie, Urrie), and the card even tells me where two of them are (Stone Wave Cliffs, Esquie's Nest). "No VP needed" is huge, because I don't understand points at all. It also says he "never gets angry" and "only fights the guards sitting on his rocks," so I'm not going to be dragged into constant fights, which is what scares me about a game with "battle" in every other sentence. And "Guards fall to anyone's battles" means other players might even clear the way for me. Worries: I don't know what "bring home" means or where home is. Does "Vagabond" mean I'm a lone wanderer with no base, and does that make me an easy target? Also "No win while he is in a coalition." If I'm someone who can be teamed up on, that might be bad. And Dorrie is blocked if the White-Haired Man's warriors stand in the ruin, which I can't control.

2. **Maelle**: "Win two factions over as allies, make no enemies." That is basically a party game: be nice to people. I can do that. "Hold your ground in Lumière with your gear intact" is also easy to picture. But it needs 10+ VP like most, and I don't know how to be "Allied" with anyone or what "Hostile" means mechanically. Also "if only one is seated: Allied with it and 15+ VP instead" confused me. I'm not sure it applies to this table.

3. **Gustave**: The card is actually the friendliest at explaining itself, with a nice story (engineer, last year, leading the expedition). "Plant Expedition Flags as you build" sounds like putting flags on a map, which is a thing I get. The win is a long road of flags from Lumière to the Monolith that "covers all three suits" (I'm guessing suits are like fox, rabbit, mouse, the three animal types on the card). But it has too many separate conditions (Chain, Arm at 3/3, 10+ VP, Beachhead, all three animals). That is a lot of plates to spin and I don't know any of the basics yet. Also "build" and "rule" again.

4. **The Paintress**: Cool villain, cool idea (hold the middle of the map, paint three portraits). But "a roost outside the Monolith, in a clearing she rules... with 2+ of her warriors" is a mouthful, and she's "bound by her own Canvas Plan" which sounds like I'll have homework I don't understand. The win also requires me to keep the middle (the Monolith), which everyone will probably fight over.

5. **Verso**: Rank letters D to S feel like a video game grade, which is kind of fun, and "take only clean fights" is a clear idea. But "damaged items" going up and down is bookkeeping I'd mess up, and the end goal is standing ALONE in the Monolith with nobody else there, with "no win while he is in a coalition." Sounds like everybody at the table would just walk in and ruin it. Also it says his rank only moves from battles, so I'd HAVE to fight to win. I'm a nervous fighter.

6. **Sciel**: Warm, nice lady, "give Lumière's children a future," I like her. But five Sanctuaries across all three animal types, plus a standing Aquafarm, plus 10+ VP, and every Sanctuary must have NO enemy pieces at all. That seems like it depends totally on other people leaving me alone. It's the biggest number of things at once, and I don't know what a Lantern is or how I light one.

**What excites me:** Esquie's whole "friendly giant carrying the expedition anywhere" thing, and the fact that I can see a finish line.

**What worries me:** I'm picking first, so I'm picking blind. Also, three of the six are "Vagabond" characters, and the screen says at most two can be seated and the table of four has exactly one. Whichever one I take, the others can't, and nobody has explained what a Vagabond is. If it turns out Vagabonds are weak I'm in trouble, but the "no VP needed" line is tempting enough that I'll risk it.

**My pick:** Esquie.
