# Sam's playtest journal

## Before the game

Four seats, standard first 4-player setup: one Cat, one Bird, one Alliance, one Vagabond. Draft order is Jo, Kit, me, Ren. I pick third, so I have to rank all six and accept that two of my favourites will probably be gone.

Quick note on me: I have hundreds of games of Root across every faction. I know nothing about the video game these characters come from. Names like Gustave, Esquie and the Paintress are just labels on cards to me. I'm judging whether this plays like Root and whether the new wins are fair and readable.

### What I understood straight away

The "plays the X" line on every card is the best thing on the screen. Gustave is the Marquise, the Paintress is the Eyrie, Sciel is the Alliance. Esquie is the Tinker, Verso is the Ranger and Maelle is the Thief. I know exactly what I'm sitting in front of. Everyone also keeps the normal 30 VP win and the dominance cards, so the old game is still underneath. Good.

The shape of the new wins is also easy to read, and I like it. Almost every one is a countdown, and the same two-step pattern repeats:
1. At the end of their turn the table is warned that the condition is met.
2. They win at the start of their next Birdsong if it still holds.

That gives the other three of us one full lap to break it. It works like a dominance-style threat that is announced openly. It's a cleaner idea than most house rules, because I always know when to panic.

Every countdown also needs "from Act II with 10+ VP". The 10 VP floor stops a player from winning on pure board state with no scoring at all. That's sensible, and it keeps these wins tied to the Root economy.

### What I did not understand

Quoting the words that lost me:

- **"Act II" / "Act III"** is on every card and never explained. I assume it's some kind of game-phase clock. I don't know what triggers it, whether it's round-based or card-based, or whether I'll see it coming. This is the biggest gap. "From Act II" tells me a window opens, but not when.
- **"Aquafarm"** (Sciel). I'm told one has to be standing on the map, but not who puts it there, what it is, or whether I can knock it down. Does Sciel build it, or is it a neutral map feature? I can't judge the win without knowing.
- **"Lantern"** (Sciel). I'm guessing it's Sympathy renamed. That's a reasonable guess, but the card doesn't say so, and it matters for how she spreads.
- **"Canvas Plan"** (Paintress). "Bound by her own Canvas Plan" sounds like the Decree, but I don't know if the rules are the same or different. Same question for **"an easel in the Monolith"**, which I'm reading as her starting roost.
- **"Overcharge"** (Gustave). I only learn that it empties the Arm. I don't know what it does for him. The card has me needing a full Arm to win and also having a button that wipes the Arm. Interesting tension, but I can't see the payoff side.
- **"Gustave's Arm gains a charge only when he starts a battle that removes an enemy piece"**: does it ever drain on its own? If it never decays, 3/3 is only three good fights. If it does decay, the card should say so.
- **"Beachhead"**, **"Chain"**, and **"Monolith's doorstep"**. I can work out Chain from the card. Beachhead is defined (a clearing next to the Monolith), but I don't know which clearings those are, because I haven't seen the map.
- **"the White-Haired Man's warriors"** (Esquie). Who is that? Is it another player's faction, a neutral army, or a bot? I'm told his warriors in Old Lumière block a rock from being picked up, which sounds important. The card doesn't tell me whose pieces they are or how I get rid of them.
- **"Soarrie appears in Act II on Verso's card"**: what is Verso's card, and what if Verso isn't seated? The card also says "or with whoever rules the Monolith". I like that last bit, since it hands a rock to the Paintress's seat, but it's confusing.
- **"On Easy, a human Verso at rank A with 2 undamaged swords counts as S."** What is Easy? A table setting, a bot mode, a handicap? It reads like a patch for a number that didn't work out, and it makes me wonder whether the S requirement is too hard.
- **"Allied" and "Hostile"** (Maelle) read like the normal Vagabond relationship track. I take those as the Law of Root, so fine.
- Small wording snag on Gustave: the flavour line says "Plant Expedition Flags as you build", but the rules text says flags go on "at the end of each of his turns". Is it when I build or at end of turn? Probably end of turn, since that's the rule line, but the two lines don't match.
- The warning test and the win test for Sciel are different. The warning needs "no enemy pieces of any kind" in each Sanctuary, but the actual win only checks "no enemy building or token", and walk-ins are ignored. I think I get it: the strict test happens once on her turn, and the lenient one is the one that has to survive the lap. It's fine, but the card should say why, or players will assume it's a mistake.

### The win conditions next to normal Root

Normal Root: you race to 30 VP and everyone can read the scoreboard, plus the dominance cards as a catch-up. These wins add a second, board-based axis. Mostly that's healthy, because it gives each faction a goal that matches how it plays:

- **Gustave** is about Marquise sprawl: building across the map, ruling connected clearings, covering all three suits. That's what a Cat player does anyway, and it now points at a destination.
- **Paintress** is about Eyrie roosts plus warriors and rule. It also lines up with what the Bird does, and "ties count, as Lords of the Forest" is a nice callback.
- **Sciel** wants five Lantern clearings with no enemy buildings or tokens, plus an Aquafarm. That is the one I'm least sure about. In vanilla the Alliance spreads where the enemy is and farms supporters. Five clearings in all three suits with no enemy building or token, in a 4-player game where the Cat is building everywhere, looks hard.
- **Esquie** is the odd one, and the one I'm most intrigued and worried by (see below).
- **Verso** has a rank track that goes up or down with every battle. Mechanically it's the most "gamey" win, but it's public, so I like it.
- **Maelle** is "make friends, don't fight, keep your gear". That's coherent for a Thief, but it depends a lot on other seats letting her in.

Things that read as fair: public information, the warning, one full lap to react, 30 VP always available.

Things I'm nervous about:

1. **Esquie has no countdown.** Everyone else gets announced at the end of their turn and wins a Birdsong later. Esquie "wins at the end of any of his turns" once the fourth rock is home. There's no lap to react. The card does tell me the rocks are public, Florrie and Urrie are under visible guards, and Urrie only appears when he holds the other three, so there is a warning of a kind. But it's a different kind of warning from every other seat's, and I'd want to know the table has a real chance to stop the last rock. Also, **"guards fall to anyone's battles"** means my own table might clear his guards for him by accident. And "no VP needed", plus "claimed rocks are never lost", means he can pursue this quietly while nobody is looking at him as a leader.
2. **The Monolith is going to be a knot.** The Paintress needs to rule it and hold a roost there. Verso needs to stand alone in it. Gustave needs a building in a clearing next to it. Soarrie goes to its ruler. Four different cards care about the same spot, so there'll be a lot of fighting around it. That might be great or it might be one seat getting dogpiled. The Paintress starts there, so she holds the strongest hand, and Verso looks like the seat that has to evict her.
3. **Verso's numbers look rough.** I did the napkin maths on the dice. Fighting a lone warrior, a +1 needs me to remove the piece and take zero damage. By my count that's roughly three fights in eight. Most of the rest are -1, because even one hit damages an item. So the rank drifts down on average when I fight defended targets, and it only climbs reliably if I hit buildings and tokens with no warriors to shoot back. It's a puzzle, and I kind of like it, but I wouldn't want to be the one stuck with it on a long game. The "Easy" clause makes me suspect the designers noticed.
4. **Sciel's five clean Sanctuaries** feels like a lot for the slowest faction in the game. It may still be OK because she can also win at 30 VP.
5. **Gustave needs four things true at once** (10 VP, Arm 3/3, a Chain covering all suits, and a Beachhead building) and then has to hold it a whole lap. That reads as hard but fair. I'd like to know whether the Arm drains, otherwise it looks easier than it sounds.

### What excites me

- Esquie's four named rocks, each with a place and a guard count. It's a rare "collect the set" objective in Root, and I'd love to try it. A Vagabond who never gets angry and only fights the guards on his rocks is a really different feel.
- The "Hold the road / hold the portraits / hold the sanctuaries" shape. It all feels like Root: control, rule, and holding territory.
- The warnings. They give the table something to talk about.

### What worries me

- Terminology without a map. I'm choosing a character without seeing where Lumière, the Monolith, Stone Wave Cliffs, Old Lumière and Esquie's Nest are. Distance matters for every one of these wins.
- Whether the Act clock is slow enough that the 10 VP floor stays meaningful.
- Whether some seats are much easier to win with than others. My gut says Paintress is the simplest to read and Verso the hardest.

### My ranking and why

1. **esquie**. The most distinct objective, a clear public checklist, and a Vagabond is the seat I'm happiest piloting. The no-VP win also means I can play quietly while the other three squabble. I'll admit part of my reason is defensive: the one-turn win with no warning scares me as an opponent, and I'd rather hold the pen myself than watch someone else collect rocks.
2. **gustave**. I know the Marquise cold, and the road win reads as "a Cat game with a destination". Everything on the card is something a Cat does naturally. I'd take this if Esquie is gone.
3. **paintress**. Simple, readable win, and Lords of the Forest is a nice touch. The Eyrie is brittle, but the test is easy to track. I'd be fine here, but 2+ warriors on each of three portraits plus the Monolith is thin and the table gets a full lap to hit them.
4. **maelle**. Low-risk, coherent for a Thief, easy gear requirement. But it relies on other seats cooperating and I don't love depending on that. It also means sitting in Gustave's home.
5. **sciel**. I love the Alliance in the abstract, but this win cuts against how the faction plays, and I don't understand the Aquafarm at all yet.
6. **verso**. By my napkin maths the rank track drifts the wrong way unless I only fight undefended pieces, and the finish means evicting whoever holds the Monolith. Cool concept, but this looks like the hardest seat to win from.

If Jo and Kit take two of the first three, I'll still be happy with one of the others, but I'd be uneasy about Esquie being in someone else's hands.

## Setup and Round 1 (I'm the Paintress, seat 3, Eyrie)

Setup: the Eyrie start is exactly the Root start I know: roost and six warriors in the corner (the Monolith, which happens to be opposite the Cat keep in Lumiere). I took the Despot (Move and Build viziers) because my win needs roosts in all three suits, and a Build vizier is the cheapest way to get them. Also I'm at 0 VP so an early Turmoil costs me nothing.

Surprise at setup: two of MY warriors are sitting in Stone Wave Cliffs (c6) as "guards" of Esquie's Florrie rock. My supply says 12/20, so they come out of my box. I have never been asked in Root to lend warriors to someone else's objective, and nobody told me. Also, when I try to Move, c6 is not offered as a place to move out of, and it's not offered as a place to Build a roost either, even though the board says I rule it. So the guards are frozen pieces and the clearing is partly off-limits to me. That's two Root rules (move from any clearing you have warriors in, build in a clearing you rule) quietly switched off for that clearing. Not explained anywhere I saw. I'd want a line on the faction board.

Turn: Added Brutal Tactics (bird) to Move and Anvil (fox) to Build. Move: 2 warriors to Visages (fox) and 2 to the Reacher (rabbit), leaving 2 in the Monolith. Build: Anvil built the fox roost at Visages, a vizier built the rabbit roost at the Reacher. Three roosts: +2 VP and drew 2 cards (third-roost bonus), exactly like real Root. Felt like Root, 100 percent. The Decree UI is clear. Move needs a count prompt, nice.

What the others did: Sciel (4) spread seedlings (sympathy) and Gustave (2) fought her at Old Lumiere and wiped her token, then built a workshop. Esquie crafted a tea and went to Stone Wave Cliffs where my guards are. Speech bubbles: Gustave talks about "whoever painted this world gave it rules", Sciel "game night run a little long". The bubbles are cute and break the fourth wall a lot, it's fine, but they read like jokes about the game, not about the lore. Mine said "Squares, turns, little wooden figures..." fun.

My own scoreboard: Paintress 2, Esquie 2, Gustave 3, Sciel 2. Monolith Number is 30 (33 minus top score of non-dominance players). I don't know what that number does yet.

Confusion: "Monolith Number 33 minus top score". Does it do anything? Does the Paintress get something when it hits zero? It's on every screen and I can't tell why.
