# Ren's playtest journal

## Before the game

Table is Jo, Kit, Sam and me, in that pick order, so I pick last and get the scraps. Standard first 4-player Root setup: Marquise, Eyrie, Woodland Alliance, one Vagabond. I read the select screen (safe spoiler mode) with that in mind. Everything below is a first read, before a single piece has hit the board.

### What reads well

- **The mapping is the best part.** Each character is bolted onto a faction whose Root identity matches the person.
  - Gustave is the engineer, so he is the Marquise de Cat, the builder who runs a road of sawmills and workshops. A road of ruled, flagged clearings is exactly what a Marquise player already does. The mod just points the Marquise engine at a destination.
  - The Paintress is the Eyrie. Her "Canvas Plan" is the Decree: rigid, self-imposed, and it hurts you if you fail it. That is a lovely fit.
  - Sciel is the Alliance, and "Twilight Harvest" is a good double meaning. Twilight is her state in the game, and she is the farmer.
- **Verso's Perfection ladder D, C, B, A, S is the real in-game mechanic**, and the rank penalties (-1 for 1-2 damaged items, -2 for 3+) are a clean way to turn Root's item damage into "did you get hit". Strikes not counting is a good call, since otherwise the crossbow would be a free ladder.
- **The rules language is mostly proper Root.**
  - It uses ruled, Allied, Hostile, coalition, dominance, Lords of the Forest, and exhausted vs damaged items.
  - "No win while he is in a coalition" for all three Vagabonds shows somebody actually knows the Vagabond coalition rule.
  - "Exhausted items count" on Maelle is the right nit to have caught. The "if only one is seated" clause shows the 2-3 player case was thought about.
- **The warning round is a fair way to do a themed win.** You announce at the end of your turn, and you win at the start of your next Birdsong, so all three opponents get a full turn to break it. That is the same tension as a dominance card or a coalition threat.
- **Maelle has to ally Gustave, or at least not get shot in his home.** Her pawn must sit in Lumière, which is the Marquise's own keep clearing, so she has to be friends with her foster brother. That one is intentional or a happy accident, and either way I love it.

### What is unclear (quoting it)

1. **"From Act II with 10+ VP."** Every countdown win leans on Acts, and this screen never says what an Act is, who triggers it, or when it flips. At the table I will need to know whether Act II is round 3 or a card.
2. **"Soarrie appears in Act II on Verso's card."** Which card? Is there a special card in the deck? And what if Verso is not even seated, since he is one of three Vagabonds? I am guessing it is a card in the deck, but it should not be a guess.
3. **"while the White-Haired Man's warriors stand in Old Lumière it cannot be picked up."** Whose warriors? At a 4-seat table nobody plays that character, so there is some neutral or scripted force on the map. I recognise the epithet, and I know who it is covering for, but I could not tell from the screen who fields those warriors or how they move.
4. **"charge Gustave's Arm ... using Overcharge empties it."** The Overcharge action is never explained. Is it a free action, a battle bonus, or a Marquise building action? It costs the very resource his win needs, so it is the central trade-off of his character and it is not on the page.
5. **Sciel's two different tests.** "A Sanctuary is a clearing with her Lantern and no enemy pieces of any kind" for the warning, but the win check is "no enemy building or token" and says "Enemy warriors or pawns that only walk in do not stop it." I think this is intentional (strict to warn, forgiving to win, so a single pawn cannot veto it), but it reads like two rules for one word. Rename the loose test or say so up front.
6. **"On Easy, a human Verso at rank A with 2 undamaged swords counts as S."** Easy for whom? Is the difficulty the whole table's or just a bot's? I assume it is a setting we will see on the new-game screen, but it is odd to read it as a character rule.
7. **"bound by her own Canvas Plan"** is never defined in the blurb. I will have to read the actual Decree-like rule to know what her handicap is, and it matters a lot to how scary she is.

### Lore check in safe mode

I finished the whole game, so I know where the bodies are buried. Overall the text is careful.

- Gustave as engineer, Maelle as Gustave's sixteen-year-old foster sister and the youngest on the Expedition, Sciel as a warm farmer type, Esquie as the big friendly carrier, Verso as the calm stranger who knows too much, Lumière, Old Lumière, Stone Wave Cliffs, Esquie's Nest, the Monolith and the Paintress painting a number each year. All of that reads right to me, and none of it spoils the ending.
- **Possible leaks for someone who has not played:** the word "Canvas" (the Paintress's "Canvas Plan" and Maelle's "A Home in the Canvas"), "The Family Portrait" and "Living Portraits" for the Paintress, "Maelle ... looking for somewhere to belong", and "in his last year" for Gustave. None of them is a full spoiler, but a newcomer who plays the Paintress will start asking "whose family?". If safe mode is meant to be airtight, those four are the lines I would look at. The Verso blurb "seems to know the Continent far too well" is exactly the right amount of hint.
- **Things I would double check rather than call wrong:** "pistol" for Verso (I remember the sword much more clearly, maybe I have forgotten a ranged skill) and "farmer-turned-teacher" for Sciel (the farmer I remember, teacher I do not). I also could not place "Aquafarm" right away, and the rock names Florrie, Dorrie, Soarrie and Urrie are new to me; they could be from a part of the game I missed. I will ask in the room rather than assume the mod got it wrong.

### Themed wins vs the 30 VP race

- All five countdown wins have a **10+ VP** floor. In a normal Marquise, Eyrie or Alliance game 10 VP is about round 3. So the floor only stops a first-turn rush; it does not make the themed wins "a 30 VP game with extra steps." The real game becomes: **who is first to put a themed meter on the table?** That is a good second axis. Instead of only "who is leading on the track", I will be tracking Gustave's Arm, the Paintress's portraits, Sciel's Lanterns, Verso's rank, Esquie's rocks.
- **Esquie is the odd one out.** "No VP needed", no warning round, win at the end of any of his turns. Every other character has to tell the table one round ahead and survive three turns. Esquie never does. The table can only stop it by not killing his guards, because "Guards fall to anyone's battles", and by sitting on the rock clearings. If everyone watches the rocks it is fine; if someone gets sloppy he wins from nowhere. That is a design choice I like, and the one I will watch hardest for balance.
- **The Monolith is the hub of everything.** The Paintress must hold it, Gustave's Chain ends at a clearing next to it, Verso must stand alone in it, and Soarrie rides with whoever rules it. All of them point at the Paintress's corner. Thematically perfect ("every expedition sets out to stop her"), but strategically it means the Eyrie seat gets dogpiled from three directions, and the Eyrie is the one faction that cannot take a dogpile (turmoil).
- **Probable ease of the countdowns, from easiest to hardest to actually pull off:**
  - Paintress and Sciel look easiest to get on the board but also the easiest to break (kill one warrior in a portrait, or drop one wood token or building on a Lantern clearing).
  - Gustave is the most coherent, because his win is just Marquise play plus three battles that remove pieces.
  - Maelle depends on aid actions and her own self-restraint.
  - Verso looks hardest. Four clean fights, then standing alone in the Eyrie capital. I think it will mostly be a 30 VP Vagabond with a nice side objective.
- **Does one side drown the other?** Not on this screen. Both ways to win stay available ("Every character can also win the Root way"), and the themed win is built from Root verbs (rule, build, battle, aid). I think the coherence is good. What I will test is whether the 10 VP floor lets a quiet player slip a theme win through before the table reacts.

### What excites me, what worries me

- **Excites:** Esquie's quest (a clean, public to-do list with a real puzzle in it), Verso's visible rank ladder (a score the table can attack), and the Gustave and Maelle link through Lumière.
- **Worries:** the Paintress seat getting piled on; unexplained Acts, Overcharge and Verso's card; Esquie's no-warning win; and Sciel's Sanctuaries being undone by any Marquise wood token (Marquise tokens are everywhere, which is the whole point of being the Marquise).

### My ranking, and why

I pick last, so I expect a Vagabond or whatever faction is left. I am ranking by "how much do I want to sit in this seat", with a weight on "can I realistically cash the themed win without being torn apart".

1. **esquie.** The only win that does not need a VP score and does not give a warning round, and the whole goal is a public checklist I can plan around. Tinker is not the strongest Vagabond, but a rock quest is a clear plan and the table will not quite know how to stop it. If I am left with all three Vagabonds this is my pick.
2. **verso.** The most faithful translation of a character mechanic, a strong fighting Vagabond in plain Root terms, and a rank the table can attack. The win itself is the hardest of the six, but the 30 VP road still works.
3. **gustave.** The Marquise engine is the best in the game, his themed win is a natural extension of what it already does, and he starts in Lumière with a head start on Maelle and Esquie. The cost is that he needs three removing battles in a faction that usually avoids fights.
4. **maelle.** Fun, with an unusual "make friends" plan, but it depends on the table letting her aid and on not being hit in Gustave's home. A thief who must not fight, in a game of fighters, is a narrow path.
5. **sciel.** A Woodland Alliance with a quiet-corners theme could be great because everyone else is fighting at the Monolith. But the Sanctuary rules are the clunkiest and the easiest for one token to break.
6. **paintress.** Iconic and strongest in flavour, but the whole table's wins run through her capital, and the Eyrie is the faction that least enjoys that. I would only choose her if I wanted to play the villain and soak up the damage.

## Round 1

Seat 4 goes first, so I opened the whole game with nothing to react to. That is odd for a last pick (normally the last seat is last), but fine; the log says "seat 4 (Sciel) takes the first turn".

- **What I did.** Spread three tokens at 1 supporter each: Old Lumière (fox hub, five paths), Gestral Village (mouse, three open slots, far from the Monolith) and Esquie's Nest (rabbit corner). Scored 0+1+1 = 2 VP. I wanted one token of each suit for crafting and as Revolt anchors, all touching c8 or c7. Then I mobilised all three cards in my hand (Sappers, Tax Collector, Cobbler) as supporters, because with 0 warriors on the map I cannot Train, organise or recruit, and I did not want a persistent-effect craft. Drew Travel Gear. Ended the turn with 3 supporters (fox, rabbit, bird) and 1 card in hand.
- **Feels like Root?** Completely. This is a proper Alliance round-one.
- **Unclear / possibly a bug:** Lumière (c1) never shows up as a spread target, even from Old Lumière, which touches it, and with a bird in my supporters. It was missing from the very first list too (c2..c12 shown, no c1). Is Lumière deliberately unspreadable? If so the rules text should say so; if not it is a missing option. I would not have minded Lumière being barred, but I want to know why.
- **Setup notes.** The Marquise garrisoned every clearing but the Monolith, the Eyrie put 6 warriors plus a roost on the Monolith, then dropped 2 more at Stone Wave Cliffs on Florrie. The Paintress's Despot leader and Loyal Viziers in Move and Build look like a rough opener for her; I will see.
- **Speech bubbles.** All four opened with a line. Gustave: "Thirty-two, one year left..." is exactly his voice (and the 32/33 joke lands). The Paintress: "Squares, turns, little wooden figures" works for her. Esquie's "I'm much too round for squares" made me laugh. Mine ("game night run a little long") is a bit meta for Sciel, but it is funny.
- **Spoiler safety.** Lines all read safe for a newcomer. No ending reveals.
- **Log observation.** When I spend supporters, the log prints the exact card (name, suit) publicly: "[2] Gently Used Knapsack (fox)". That matches Root (spent cards go to the discard face-up), so it is rules-correct, but note that my remaining supporters stay hidden.

## Round 2

- **What happened to me first.** On his turn Gustave battled my lone Old Lumière token with his one warrior, took it off for 1 VP (I got the Outrage card), then built a workshop on top of my old spot for 2 VP. My retort in the bubble: "Those were seedlings. You can pay the farmer on your way out." Good line, and it was a perfectly Root play by a Marquise (hit the empty token, score, build). Rules check: defenseless gives the attacker +1 hit, which is right.
- **What the others did.** Esquie crafted Root Tea (2 VP) and took tea back with Day Labor (nice use of a Vagabond verb). The Paintress's Despot dropped two roosts (Visages c9, The Reacher c11) with 2 warriors each, scored 2 VP on the Roosts track, and now reads "Portraits 2/3 ... ONE STEP AWAY" on the goal tracker. That is the table's loudest alarm. She still needs the mouse portrait (the Monolith) plus 10 VP plus Act II, so not yet.
- **What I did.** Revolted at Esquie's Nest (rabbit + a bird, which killed Gustave's lone warrior): rabbit base, 1 warrior that became my officer. Spread a third token to Visages (c9) with my last fox, scoring 1. Crafted Travel Gear off the rabbit token (Boots, +1 VP, and it denies Boots from the supply). Evening: recruited a warrior at the base. Hand 2 (Favor of the Mice, Command Warren), 1 bird supporter, 4 VP.
- **Why Visages?** It carries one of the Paintress's two portraits and her roost. Next turn, if I can find 2 fox or bird supporters, a Revolt there would clear her roost and warriors, wreck the portrait and bring a base to the middle. It is a threat on the board even before I do it.
- **Unclear.** The Aquafarm. I see "Moon: 0 Aquafarm(s) on the map" on the goal tracker but I have no option anywhere (Birdsong, Daylight, Evening) that builds one. I do not know whether it is a base, a ruin, a card or a token. I need that explained before I could ever finish the themed win.
- **Possible rules break.** When my revolt removed Gustave's warrior the log said "(not their turn, decided by the default): Field Hospitals ... Spend a rabbit card to place them at the keep? -> Do not use Field Hospitals". In real Root, Field Hospitals is a Marquise card-ability that works whenever warriors are removed, any turn. The sim let a player be auto-skipped on someone else's turn. If Gustave had a rabbit card in hand he lost a legal rescue without being asked. Nothing mandatory, but the 'decided by the default' path should not skip a real choice.
- **Root or Expedition 33?** All Root this round. Only the bubbles carried the Expedition 33 flavour. The Monolith Number now reads 30, which is a nice heartbeat (33 minus top score).
