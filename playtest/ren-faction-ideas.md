# Root 33: one faction per character (Ren's pitch)

Ren, seat 4 in playtest game 1 (I played Sciel). I wrote this after three rounds and after reading Jo's, Kit's and Sam's journals. I've played every Root faction, I know the Law of Root, and I've finished Expedition 33. Anything that leans on late Act II or Act III is marked **[SPOILER]** so you can cut a safe version.

## What the table felt

All four of us said the same thing in different words.

- **Kit:** "still 90/10 Root ... The numbers are doing the talking and the characters are doing the greetings."
- **Jo:** "mostly like Root, with Expedition 33 in the speech bubbles and place names."
- **Sam:** the bubbles "read like jokes about the game, not about the lore."
- **Me, round 3:** "Zero Expedition 33 on the board; it only shows in the goal tracker and bubbles."

Before the game I said the mapping was the best part, and I still think it was clever. But a mapping is exactly the ceiling. My Sciel was a Woodland Alliance with a renamed base (the "Aquafarm"). I spent my turn on revolt maths, not reading anybody's future. Below, each character gets its own engine instead, built from how they fight in Expedition 33.

## Ground rules I held every faction to

- **The Root skeleton stays.**
  - Every faction plays Birdsong, Daylight, Evening.
  - Everyone uses the shared deck and crafts by suit, using its own named crafting pieces.
  - Battles use the two dice: the attacker takes the high die, the defender the low one. Hits are capped, a defenceless defender takes +1, and ambush cards work as usual.
  - Rule means most warriors plus buildings. Pawns don't rule unless a faction says so.
  - Players win with 30 VP or a dominance card. Factions with no warriors use the Vagabond coalition rule. Nobody is eliminated.
- **One new noun per faction.** Jo and Kit drowned in "ruled", "roost", "Birdsong", "Aquafarm". A bespoke faction costs a new word, so each one gets one big word (the Arm, the Number, Stains) and nothing else new.
- **Every special win is a countdown with the warning lap.** That now includes Esquie. Unless noted, a special win needs Act II and 10+ VP.
- **Print the Act clock on the score track:** Act II when anyone reaches 10 VP, Act III at 20. Sam worked out the 10 from the log. It should never have needed working out.
- **Root setup tags.** Each faction carries a Reach number and a Militant or Insurgent tag, so the Law of Root setup still works. Four seats want Reach 21+ and at least one militant. My numbers are first guesses.
- **Safe mode and spoiler mode both stay.**

## The eleven

### 1. Gustave: The Expedition

- **Pitch.** One boat and no reinforcements. A march of Expedition Flags from Lumière to the Monolith, powered by an arm that stores lightning for one perfect strike.
- **Board.**
  - Pieces: Gustave's pawn, 14 expeditioners (warriors), 8 Expedition Flags, and the Arm track (0-10).
  - Everything starts in Lumière, which is flagged. There is no keep and no garrison. That also answers my round-1 question about why nobody could enter Lumière.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** Rest. Every wounded expeditioner in a flagged clearing stands up. Draw 1 card per 3 flags.
  - **Daylight:** 3 actions, chosen from March (two moves), Battle, Plant a Flag (discard a card of the clearing's suit in a clearing he rules) and Craft (flags are his crafting pieces). Every action puts 1 charge on the Arm.
  - **Evening:** draw 1, then score.
- **The new thing: an army that can never recruit.**
  - A dead expeditioner is gone for good, unless it dies in a flagged clearing. There it is laid down as wounded (it doesn't rule and deals no hits) and stands up at the next Rest.
  - **Overcharge:** once per turn, in Gustave's clearing, he empties the Arm into a battle for +1 hit per 3 charges, on top of the dice.
- **From Expedition 33.**
  - His charge-building skills and Overcharge. The game caps his charges at 10, and so does this Arm.
  - Flags are where you rest and the fallen get back up.
  - An Expedition that sails out and doesn't come home.
- **Scoring.**
  - A flag scores by depth when it is planted: 1 VP near Lumière, 2 VP mid-map, 3 VP at the Monolith's doorstep.
  - An Overcharge that removes 3 or more pieces scores +2 VP.
- **Special win: The Road** (kept from the current version).
  - A connected chain of flags from Lumière to a clearing next to the Monolith, covering all three suits, with the Arm at 10.
  - That means he must sit on a loaded arm instead of firing it.
  - Warn the table, survive a lap, then win.
- **Table.**
  - **Helps:** Esquie can carry his walkers, and Maelle respawns at his flags.
  - **Hunts:** the Paintress's Easels.
  - **Fears:** attrition, because every dead walker is permanent; anything that erases; one burned flag cutting the Road.
- **Complexity 2. Newcomer seat.** Charge, charge, boom. Kit already felt that rhythm from "Arm 1/3" on the tracker; here it is the whole faction.
- **[SPOILER]** Gustave doesn't survive Act I. Spoiler module: if the White-Haired Man removes Gustave's pawn, the faction flips to its **Legacy** side. The player keeps playing; nobody is eliminated.
  - There is no more Overcharge.
  - Each flag scores 1 VP every Evening and still revives the wounded.
  - The Arm's charge passes to Maelle if she is seated and puts her straight into Virtuose.

### 2. Maelle: The Duelist of Lumière

- **Pitch.** A lone fencer who changes stance mid-fight, gets sharper each time someone swings at her, and decides for herself which factions are family.
- **Board.**
  - Pieces: Maelle's pawn, a Stance dial (Offensive, Defensive, Virtuose), an AP track (0-9), and 3 Ribbons. She has no warriors, no items and no relationship track.
  - AP is her health. Every hit she takes costs 1 AP. At 0 she is knocked out to Lumière (or the nearest flag) and skips her next Daylight.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** gain 2 AP and set her stance. She can choose Virtuose only if she ended last turn having won a battle in Offensive.
  - **Daylight:** spend AP. Move costs 1, Battle 2, Lunge 3 (move, then battle). Ribbon costs 1: pin one on another faction's piece in her clearing. Craft costs 1: her clearing is a crafting piece of its suit, plus 1 more per Ribbon there.
  - **Evening:** draw 1, then score.
- **The new thing: a stance dial that rewrites the battle maths for both sides, and she can step into other people's fights.**
  - **Offensive:** she deals +1 hit and takes +1 hit.
  - **Defensive:** the first hit on her in each battle is parried and becomes +1 AP. She may also join as defender in any battle in her clearing against a faction that wears her Ribbon.
  - **Virtuose:** her hits equal both dice added together (capped at 4). Afterwards she drops to Defensive.
- **From Expedition 33.**
  - Maelle's three stances, the skills that switch between them, and Virtuose as her big payoff.
  - The foster sister looking for somewhere to belong.
- **Scoring.**
  - 1 VP per hit she deals in Virtuose.
  - 2 VP the first time she ribbons each faction.
  - Each Evening, 1 VP per ribboned faction she defended this round.
- **Special win: A Place to Belong** (safe name; the spoiler name is *A Home in the Canvas*).
  - She has Ribbons on two factions, and neither has attacked her this Act.
  - She stands in Lumière, in Defensive stance, with 6+ AP.
  - Countdown: warn, survive the lap, win.
- **Table.**
  - **Helps:** factions court her Ribbon, and the leader learns that poking her only feeds her.
  - **Hunts:** whoever hits her family.
  - **Fears:** being ignored (no fights near her means no AP), and several small attacks drawing her AP down in one round.
- **Complexity 3. A confident newcomer or a veteran.** It is one dial and one number, but she needs table sense.
- **[SPOILER]** Maelle is Alicia.
  - From Act III she can **Repaint**: for 3 AP, return a removed piece of a faction she has ribboned to its supply.
  - Her countdown becomes "keep the world". She can declare it only while the White-Haired Man's erase win or Verso's ending is live, which puts the finale fight on the table.

### 3. Lune: The Stainweaver

- **Pitch.** A scholar-mage who marks the map with elemental Stains, detonates them in pairs from a distance, and reads the journals of every expedition that failed before hers.
- **Board.**
  - Pieces: Lune's pawn, 16 Stains (Fire, Ice, Lightning, Earth), 2 Light Stains (wild), a Grimoire grid of combos, and a Journal slot.
  - She is fragile. If she is hit twice in one turn, she is routed to the nearest ruin.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** Study. If she is at a ruin, she takes its item face down as a Journal, worth 2 VP; nobody else gets that item.
  - **Daylight:** 3 casts.
    - **Stain:** discard a card to put a Stain in her clearing or an adjacent one. Fox gives Fire, rabbit gives Earth, mouse gives Lightning, bird gives Ice.
    - **Detonate:** consume two different Stains in one clearing within range 1 (range 2 if she spends a Light). This is a battle there without being there: she rolls, deals the high die plus the combo's bonus, and takes no hits.
  - **Evening:** draw 1, plus 1 per completed Grimoire row.
- **The new thing: neutral terrain.** Stains aren't hers. They change the clearing for everyone in it.
  - **Fire:** defenders take +1 hit.
  - **Earth:** buildings ignore the first hit.
  - **Lightning:** the attacker rolls a third die and drops one.
  - **Ice:** nobody can build or craft there.
  - In any battle in a stained clearing, the first hit can be spent wiping a Stain instead.
  - Stains count as crafting pieces of their suit.
- **From Expedition 33.**
  - Lune's Stains: one spell makes them and the next one consumes them.
  - Her obsession with the journals of earlier expeditions (her parents were on one).
- **Scoring.**
  - 1-3 VP per combo the first time she casts it, ticked off on the Grimoire.
  - 1 VP per piece removed by a detonation.
  - 2 VP per Journal.
- **Special win: Read the Monolith.**
  - All four elements are stained across the clearings around the Monolith, and Lune stands next to it.
  - Countdown. One battle anywhere in the ring can wipe a Stain and break it.
- **Table.** She is the table's weather.
  - **Helps:** Gustave wants Fire in front of his Overcharge. The Paintress wants Earth under her Easels. Everyone curses Ice.
  - **Hunts:** whoever is crowded.
  - **Fears:** fast armies walking onto her pawn, and erasure of her stained clearings.
  - Her closest cousin at the table is Sciel, since both mark clearings. That fits; they're friends in the game. Keep Stains about places and Foretell about people.
- **Complexity 4. Veteran.** The stain map is a lot of state for everyone to track, not only her.

### 4. Sciel: Foretell and Twilight

- **Pitch.** The smiling farmer who deals tarot onto her rivals' futures, alternates Sun and Moon, and harvests every Foretell on the map in one Twilight.
- **Board.**
  - Pieces: Sciel's pawn, 6 Lanterns (buildings that rule, and this time they're honestly hers), a Sun track (0-3), a Moon track (0-3), and Foretell stacks (cards from her hand laid face up beside a clearing).
- **Turn.**
  - **Daylight:** 3 actions. Each is a Sun action or a Moon action, and it moves that track.
    - **Sun, Foretell:** lay a card on a named enemy faction in a clearing her Lanterns light (a Lantern clearing or one next to it). Maximum 3 per faction per clearing.
    - **Sun, Move.**
    - **Moon, Tend:** place a Lantern where she stands by paying a card of its suit.
    - **Moon, Give:** hand a card to another player, no strings attached and no relationship track.
    - **Moon, Rest:** draw a card.
  - **Evening:** 1 VP per Lantern in a clearing with no enemy building. Draw 1, plus 1 per 2 Lanterns. Lanterns are her crafting pieces.
- **The new thing: Twilight.**
  - When Sun and Moon are both at 3 at the end of her turn, the table is told that her next Birdsong is Twilight.
  - In Twilight she **Harvests**. Every stack resolves as a battle in which only she deals hits: the high die plus the stack size. She scores 1 VP per piece removed, the cards go to the discard pile, and both tracks reset.
  - A Foretell lit by a Lantern that gets destroyed scatters. A faction that leaves the clearing sheds it.
  - She can't just attack. Half of her turn has to be kindness (Moon) or she never reaches Twilight.
- **From Expedition 33.**
  - Foretell stacks laid on enemies and consumed later, and Sun and Moon charges merging into Twilight.
  - The tarot look. Kit went looking for it on the current card and didn't find it.
  - The warm farmer who is harder than her smile.
- **Scoring:** the Lantern score each Evening, plus the Harvest score.
- **Special win: Twilight Harvest** (name kept).
  - At the start of a Twilight, she has 2+ stacks on every other seated faction and Lanterns in all three suits.
  - The Twilight announcement is the warning, so the table always gets its lap. Break one Lantern or walk out of one clearing to stop it.
- **Table.**
  - Every player can read their own doom stacked beside their clearings and has to choose: leave the clearing, or go and burn a Lantern.
  - **Helps:** her Moon gifts make her the trailing players' friend, so the table can't simply gang up on her.
  - **Hunts:** the leader's biggest stacks.
  - **Fears:** armies that walk straight through (Gustave, Monoco), and anything that erases her lit clearings.
- **Complexity 3. Card lovers, newcomers included.**

### 5. Monoco: The Bestial Wheel

- **Pitch.** A Gestral who learns to fight by beating things. Every skill he uses turns the Wheel, and the Wheel decides which trick is hot.
- **Board.**
  - Pieces: Monoco's pawn and 8 Gestrals (warriors).
  - The Bestial Wheel shows five masks (Agile, Heavy, Caster, Balanced, Almighty) and has a pointer.
  - His skill rack holds three base skills plus the Trophies he learns.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** Gestrals removed last round come back from the Sacred River and stand with Monoco.
  - **Daylight:** up to 3 skills. His base skills are Stomp (battle), Hop (move 2, Gestrals included) and Jeer (place 1 Gestral where he stands). Each skill turns the Wheel one step. If the skill's mask matches the pointer, its bonus fires: an extra hit, an extra move, or a card. Almighty matches anything.
  - **Evening:** draw 1, then score.
- **The new thing: Trophies.**
  - When his battle clears the last piece of a faction out of a clearing, he takes that faction's Trophy, once per faction.
  - A Trophy is a new skill with a mask, themed on the loser:
    - Gustave's: a mini-Overcharge.
    - The Paintress's: paint a Gestral next to an Easel.
    - Lune's: leave a Stain behind.
  - Ruins hold Nevron Trophies.
  - Each Trophy is a crafting piece of the suit where he won it.
  - The order of his skills becomes a new puzzle every turn.
- **From Expedition 33.**
  - The Bestial Wheel and its mask matching.
  - His skills, borrowed from the Nevrons he has beaten.
  - Gestrals coming back from the Sacred River, and Gestral Village's love of a good duel.
- **Scoring.**
  - 2 VP per new Trophy.
  - 1 VP per Almighty match.
  - Normal VP for removing pieces.
- **Special win: A Full Wheel.**
  - He holds a Trophy from every other seated faction and stands in Gestral Village with 3+ Gestrals.
  - Esquie's Trophy can only be given as a gift, never taken. Esquie can trade it for a ride.
  - Countdown.
- **Table.**
  - He must fight everyone at least once, so he is the roaming brawler who keeps the table honest. He is also the threat the leader loves to point at someone else.
  - **Helps:** Monoco and Verso never have to battle each other. Sharing a clearing with Verso turns Monoco's Wheel one step for free.
  - **Hunts:** a little of everyone, since he needs every Trophy.
  - **Fears:** turtles (no clearing he can empty) and Ice.
- **Complexity 3.** A newcomer who likes brawling can just fight; a veteran squeezes the Wheel.

### 6. Verso: Perfection

- **Pitch.** A calm, immortal swordsman graded on every fight. He can't die, but every hit costs him rank, and rank is his whole engine.
- **Board:** Verso's pawn and the Perfection ladder (D, C, B, A, S). Nothing else.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** he knows the way. He looks at the top 2 cards of the deck and keeps 1 on top.
  - **Daylight:** his rank sets his actions: D 2, C 3, B 3, A 4, S 4. He may Move (forest paths count as paths for him), Battle or Craft.
  - **Crafting:** at B he counts as one crafting piece of his clearing's suit, at A as two, at S as three.
  - **Evening:** draw 1, then score.
- **The new thing: rank is his actions, his hits and his health at once.**
  - His maximum hits are D 1, C 2, B 2, A 3, S 4.
  - Every hit he takes drops him one step. A hit at D kills him. He comes back at the next Birdsong at any ruin or Lumière, still at D.
  - He climbs a step for every battle in which he deals more hits than he takes.
  - **Parry:** when he is attacked, he may discard a card of the clearing's suit to cancel one hit and deal one back. Each parry also climbs a step.
- **From Expedition 33.**
  - The Perfection rank, which rises with clean damage and drops when he is hit.
  - The parry and his immortality.
  - The man who "seems to know the Continent far too well".
- **Scoring.**
  - 1 VP per battle won at A or S.
  - 2 VP each time he reaches S.
- **Special win: Alone in the Monolith** (kept).
  - He is at rank S and is the only piece in the Monolith.
  - Warn the table. He wins at his Birdsong if he is still at S and still alone.
  - Drop the "Easy" patch. Sam's dice maths showed the old rule (+1 only on a zero-damage fight, -1 for any damaged item) drifting downward. "More hits than you take" fixes the drift without a difficulty clause.
- **Table.**
  - Killing him only slows him, so the table polices him by poking. Any single hit denies S for a turn, and everyone can chip in, which is very Root.
  - **Helps:** Monoco, his old friend.
  - **Hunts:** the Paintress. He is the knife pointed at the Monolith.
  - **Fears:** a crowded Monolith, and death by a thousand ambushes.
- **Complexity 2. Newcomer hero seat.** He was Kit's first pick, and Kit would understand him in one turn.
- **[SPOILER]** Who he is to the Paintress, to Maelle and to the White-Haired Man. Spoiler module: in Act III Verso secretly picks an ending card, revealed when it matters.
  - **Let it end:** he also wins if the White-Haired Man's erase win resolves.
  - **Stay:** he scores alongside Maelle's countdown instead.

### 7. Esquie: Esquie's Wanderings

- **Pitch.** A giant who never fights, carries anyone who wants a lift, and reopens the map one recovered friend at a time.
- **Board.**
  - Esquie's pawn, which can't be removed.
  - The Traversal track: Walk, Swim, Smash, Dive, Fly.
  - The four rock friends (kept from the current version) and a Snack pile.
- **Map.**
  - Setup closes a few paths: water (needs Swim), rockfall (needs Smash), deep water (needs Dive), and one sky route to the Monolith (needs Fly).
  - When Esquie crosses a closed path, it opens for everyone, for good.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** Snack. He may discard a card matching his clearing for +1 move this turn.
  - **Daylight:** move up to 3 using his traversal. He may carry any other pieces in his clearing whose owner says yes, as far as he goes. He may pick up a rock in a clearing with no guards.
  - **Evening:** draw 1, then score.
- **The new thing: progress that opens the map for everybody, and VP for helping.**
  - He scores 1 VP per faction he carried this turn (maximum 2).
  - He cannot battle. If he is hit, he is knocked over: he drops his passengers and any rock, and loses a move next turn. The attacker scores nothing for it.
  - Each rock he brings home is a crafting piece of the suit it came from.
- **From Expedition 33.**
  - Esquie is the overworld itself: swim, smash, dive and fly, unlocked by bringing his friends' stones home.
  - He carries the Expedition anywhere and never gets angry.
- **Rocks.**
  - Guards are neutral pieces now, not out of the Paintress's box (Sam's complaint, and a fair one).
  - Anyone's battles clear guards, but Esquie can't. So he needs friends, and he pays them back in rides.
- **Scoring:** rides, plus 2 VP per rock home.
- **Special win: Bring Them Home** (kept, still no VP floor).
  - It now gets the same warning lap as everyone else. When he lifts the last rock, he announces it.
  - He wins at his next Birdsong if the rock is still riding with him. Knock him over to break it.
- **Table.**
  - **Helps:** everyone. He is the table's taxi and nobody's enemy, and that is exactly why the table has to watch him: every ride is a point, and refusing a ride costs you tempo.
  - **Hunts:** nobody.
  - **Fears:** guards nobody will clear, and the White-Haired Man unmaking clearings on his route.
- **Complexity 1. Newcomer seat.** Jo understood him from the box text alone.

### 8. The Paintress: The Number

- **Pitch.** She never leaves the Monolith. Every Evening she paints a lower Number, and anyone who has grown past it is erased.
- **Board.**
  - **The Monolith:** her fixed home. It can't be moved or removed, but it can take hits.
  - **6 Easels:** buildings, called Portraits in spoiler mode.
  - **16 Nevrons:** warriors.
  - **The Number track,** from 33 down to 0. This is the current "Monolith Number", finally doing something.
  - The Easels and the Monolith are her crafting pieces.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** Paint. Discard cards; each card paints a Nevron into a clearing of its suit (bird counts as any) that is the Monolith, is next to it, or holds an Easel.
  - **Daylight:** battle once in each clearing with Nevrons, and raise Easels in clearings she rules (each costs a card of its suit). **She has no Move action at all.**
  - **Evening:** lower the Number by 1, or by 2 if she has Easels in all three suits. Then the Gommage.
- **The new thing: score is age.**
  - **The Gommage:** every faction whose VP is at or above the Number removes 2 pieces of its choice. Pawn factions lose 2 of their own resource instead (AP, rank, Chroma and so on). She scores 1 VP per faction hit.
  - **Pushing back:** each Easel destroyed raises the Number by 2, and each hit on the Monolith raises it by 1.
  - Nothing in Root works like this: an army that never moves, painted onto the board, and a doom clock tied to the score track.
- **From Expedition 33.**
  - The Gommage and the number painted lower every year.
  - The Monolith, and Nevrons appearing where she paints.
- **Scoring:** the Gommage VP, plus 1 VP for each Easel in a suit she didn't have yet.
- **Special win: The Last Number.**
  - At the end of her turn, the Number is at or below every other faction's score. Warn the table; she wins at her Birdsong if it still holds.
  - No VP floor is needed, because the table's own scores are the fuse.
  - The youngest player (the lowest score) is the last protection. That is thematically brutal and mechanically very Root.
- **Table.** Every expedition sets out to stop her.
  - **Hunts:** the leader. The Gommage taxes whoever is ahead, so whoever is behind quietly likes her. That politics is the Root table I want.
  - **Fears:** Verso at the Monolith, Gustave walking at her Easels, Lune ringing the Monolith, and a table that coordinates.
- **Complexity 3. Veteran villain seat.** Sam played the Eyrie and enjoyed being the target.
- **[SPOILER]** Who she is, and whose hand the brush really ends up in.
  - Spoiler module: in Act III, if the White-Haired Man is seated, he can take the Number for a round. It then moves on his turn instead of hers.
  - Spoiler mode renames Easels to Portraits and adds "The Family Portrait" as her achievement. In my pre-game notes I flagged "Family Portrait", "Living Portraits" and "Canvas" as leaks in safe text, so keep them out of it.

### 9. The Curator: The Quiet Workshop

- **Pitch.** A masked craftsman who takes no side in battle. He decides whose blade gets sharper, and the war pays him in Chroma.
- **Board.**
  - The Curator's pawn rides with the **Camp**, a tile that sits in a **forest**, never in a clearing.
  - Chroma tokens and 8 Hallmarks.
  - The Camp counts as a crafting piece of every suit it touches.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** move the Camp to a neighbouring forest.
  - **Daylight:** Temper and Craft.
    - **Temper:** spend Chroma to put a Hallmark on a crafted item or persistent card belonging to any player whose pieces touch the Camp. The upgrade: the item counts as two, or the card's effect gets +1.
    - **Craft:** as normal, using the Camp.
  - **Evening:** draw, then score.
- **The new thing: he lives between clearings, profits from fights he isn't in, and makes other factions stronger.**
  - He gains 1 Chroma whenever a piece is removed in any clearing touching the Camp.
  - Every time a Hallmarked card is used, he scores 1 VP (at most 1 per owner per round).
  - **Raiding the Camp:** anyone in an adjacent clearing can attack it in a normal two-dice battle. He defends with the low die, capped at 2. Each hit costs him a Chroma, and at 0 Chroma the Camp scatters to a forest of the raider's choice.
- **From Expedition 33:** the Curator at camp, upgrading weapons with Chroma Catalysts. Quiet, masked, and he knows more than he says.
- **Scoring:** Hallmark VP, plus 2 VP for each new faction he tempers for.
- **Special win: Masterwork.**
  - Hallmarks sit on cards of every other seated faction at once, and nobody has raided the Camp since his last turn.
  - Warn the table, survive the lap, win.
  - To break it, raid the Camp, or discard your own Hallmarked item, which is a real cost.
- **Table.**
  - Everyone wants his upgrades, and nobody wants him to win. He is the quiet kingmaker who needs fights next to him but never on him.
  - **Helps:** the trailing players, because they need the help most.
  - **Hunts:** nobody directly. He wants fights next to his forest.
  - **Fears:** a peaceful table (no Chroma), and anyone who parks an army next to his forest.
- **Complexity 4. Veteran,** and someone comfortable with table talk.
- **[SPOILER]** He is a painted copy of the White-Haired Man, on the world's side. Spoiler module: once per game he can cancel one of the White-Haired Man's erasures ("I know his brushwork").

### 10. Clea: The Studio Beyond

- **Pitch.** A painter who never sets foot on the map. She designs a handful of custom monsters, writes each one an instinct, and lets them loose from the edge of the world.
- **Board.**
  - A Studio board and no pawn.
  - 4 Creation figures, each with a blank Creation card.
  - Trait tiles: Armored, Winged, Swift, Brutal, Mimic, Burrower.
  - Instinct tiles: Hunt the largest stack, Guard, March on the Monolith, Chase the leader, Return to the edge.
  - Ink.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** turn cards into Ink: 1 per card, or 2 if it matches a creation's colour.
  - **Daylight:**
    - **Design:** slot a trait onto a creation (maximum 3 traits each).
    - **Release:** place a creation in a clearing at the edge of the map or at a ruin.
    - **Command:** move and battle with one creation directly, twice per turn.
  - **Evening:** every creation acts on its Instinct, in order. Then she may swap one Instinct for 1 Ink. Draw, then score.
- **The new thing: a few custom units with their own stats and their own programming.**
  - A creation is one piece. It deals hits as 2 warriors, soaks 1 hit (plus 1 per Armored trait) and counts as 2 for rule. Traits change how it moves and fights.
  - The Eyrie programs its whole faction by suit. Clea programs individual monsters by behaviour, and builds them first.
  - Each creation on the map is a crafting piece of its clearing's suit.
- **From Expedition 33:** Clea's creations, a painter's work loosed on the Continent. I won't swear from memory which creatures in the game are hers; the mechanic doesn't depend on it.
- **Scoring.**
  - 1 VP per piece a creation removes.
  - 2 VP the first time each creation gets its third trait.
  - Each Evening, 1 VP per clearing a creation rules.
- **Special win: The Exhibition.**
  - Four creations with three traits each are on the map, ruling clearings of all three suits.
  - Warn the table, survive the lap, win.
  - Kill one creation to break it. Each one is a big investment, and that is her weakness.
- **Table.**
  - **Helps:** nobody on purpose, but her monsters are chaos the table can steer. Players learn to stand where an Instinct will lead the monsters into the leader.
  - **Hunts:** whatever her Instincts name (the Paintress's Easels in spoiler mode).
  - **Fears:** focused fire, and Monoco, who wants her creations' Trophies.
- **Complexity 5. Veterans only.** Seat her last.
- **[SPOILER]**
  - Clea is the eldest Dessendre daughter, working from outside. Even her name is a late-game reveal, so in safe mode she needs a neutral title such as "the Painter Beyond".
  - Spoiler module: in Act III her creations may enter at clearings the White-Haired Man has unmade.

### 11. The White-Haired Man: The Erasure

- **Pitch.** A silent old man who walks the Continent unmaking it. What he removes never comes back, and the clearings he empties are wiped off the map.
- **Board.**
  - His pawn. It can't be removed, like the Vagabond's.
  - 6 Blank tiles and an Exhaustion track.
  - Each Blank is a crafting piece of the suit it erased.
- **Turn.**
  - **Birdsong:** clear his Exhaustion.
  - **Daylight:** 2 actions in Act I, 3 in Act II, 4 in Act III, minus his Exhaustion.
    - **Walk:** move 1, ignoring rule.
    - **Erase:** battle. He deals hits up to the Act number plus 1. Whatever he removes goes back in the box, not to its owner's supply, so those pieces are lost for good.
    - **Unmake:** if he is alone in a clearing with no buildings, he covers it with a Blank. That clearing is gone: nobody can enter it, paths through it close, and its slots and suit disappear.
  - **Evening:** draw 1, then score.
- **The new thing: permanent loss and a shrinking map.**
  - Hits on him never remove him. Each hit adds 1 Exhaustion, which means one fewer action next turn.
  - **Repaint:** any faction with pieces next to a Blank may discard 3 cards of that clearing's suit to bring it back. The world is saved by the table spending cards.
- **From Expedition 33:** the white-haired man on the beach who ends Expedition 33's landing, and an erasure that leaves nothing behind.
- **Scoring.**
  - 1 VP per piece erased (maximum 3 per turn).
  - 3 VP per clearing unmade.
- **Special win: The Last Stroke** (safe name; the spoiler name is *Erase the Canvas*).
  - In Act III, 4 clearings are unmade and he stands in the Monolith.
  - Warn the table, survive the lap, win. Repaint one Blank or crowd the Monolith to break it.
- **Table.** He is the shared fear. The defence against him is Root's oldest lesson: keep pieces everywhere, because he can only unmake an empty clearing.
  - **Helps:** nobody.
  - **Hunts:** the Expedition seats in Act I and the Monolith in Act III.
  - **Fears:** crowded maps and a table that pools its cards.
- **Complexity 4.** A simple turn with heavy politics. Veterans only, at 4+ seats.
- **When he isn't seated:** keep him as the scripted hazard the current build has, but say so on screen. That was my round-1 question, and Kit's and Sam's too.
- **[SPOILER]** He is Renoir, the father, come to end the painted world and bring his family home. He ends Gustave in Act I, and Lumière at the end of Act II. Spoiler modules:
  - He can target Gustave's pawn for the Legacy flip.
  - He can take the Paintress's Number in Act III.
  - Clea, and Verso with "Let it end", score with him.

## Which four I would build first

**Gustave, the Paintress, Sciel and Esquie.**

1. **They make a complete, legal Root table.** There are two militants (Gustave, the Paintress) and two insurgents (Sciel, Esquie). Reach is 9 + 7 + 3 + 2 = 21, exactly the four-seat threshold.
2. **They play the story's opening and stay spoiler-safe.** The Expedition marches at the Monolith, a fortune teller reads the war, and the big friend carries everyone across the map. Nobody needs Clea, the Curator's identity or a White-Haired Man seat.
3. **They suit a mixed table.** Gustave (2) and Esquie (1) are newcomer seats; Sciel (3) and the Paintress (3) are mid seats. Jo and Kit could both sit at this table on day one.
4. **They test the four riskiest new ideas:**
   - an army that never recruits;
   - a doom clock tied to score;
   - Foretells laid on rivals and cashed in on a public timer;
   - a map that opens up.

   If those four hold, the other seven are variations on proven parts.
5. **They are the cheapest to build.** The Arm tracker, the Monolith Number, the Lanterns and the four rocks are already on screen.

**After that:**
- **Wave two:** Verso, Monoco, Maelle and Lune. These are the duelists and the mage, plus the Verso and Monoco friendship.
- **Wave three:** the Curator, Clea and the White-Haired Man. They are the heaviest in decisions taken on other players' turns, the heaviest in spoilers, and the biggest kingmaking risk.

## The three biggest risks

1. **The board empties and stops feeling like Root.**
   - Of the eleven, only Gustave, the Paintress, Monoco and Clea hold ground with a real army. The rest are pawns, tokens and tracks.
   - Root lives on ruling clearings, building slots and Lords of the Forest ties. A table of Verso, Maelle, Esquie and the Curator would have almost nothing to rule.
   - Fix: add a setup rule like Root's militant rule, so every table seats at least two of those four map-holders, and use the Reach numbers.
   - Cap the unremovable pawns (Esquie, Verso, the White-Haired Man) at two per table.
2. **Doom clocks turn the table into co-op against one player, and the Monolith gets dogpiled.**
   - The Number, the White-Haired Man's unmaking and Sciel's announced Twilight all say "stop this or lose".
   - One of them per table is Root's dominance-card tension. Two is a co-op game.
   - My pre-game worry gets worse, not better. The Road, Verso, Lune's ring and the Paintress all point at the Monolith, and Sam saw the same knot.
   - Kingmaking needs Law of Root eyes too: Hallmarks, rides and gifts all hand VP across the table.
   - Fix: don't seat the Paintress and the White-Haired Man together below five players, and keep at least one special win per table pointed away from the Monolith.
3. **Rules load and decisions on other players' turns.**
   - Jo and Kit already stalled on "ruled", "Birdsong" and "Aquafarm", and eleven bespoke factions means eleven rulebooks.
   - These designs ask questions on other players' turns: Maelle's guard, Verso's parry, choosing what the Gommage takes, raiding the Camp, Repaint.
   - The current build already answers Gustave's Field Hospitals for him ("not their turn, decided by the default"). It also burned one of my operations when I looked at Move and then skipped.
   - If the engine can't ask the right player the right question in the middle of someone else's turn, half these factions break.
   - Fix that first. Hold to one new noun per faction, and let the goal tracker do the teaching.
